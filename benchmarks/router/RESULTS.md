### Recommendation: gemma4:e4b, prompt variant v2

1. **Hard recall (the costly error) decides it.** gemma/v2 routed 0 of 31 hard
   tasks to the local model (recall 100%); gemma/v1 misrouted 1 (t60). Both Laya
   variants misrouted 4 hard tasks (recall 87.1%). On the 14 borderline items,
   Laya's hard recall is 50% (4 of 8 missed) versus 100% (gemma/v2) and 87.5%
   (gemma/v1). Laya's misses all have an "easy" verb over hard substance:
   t55 "Summarise the current state of quantum error correction...", t56
   "Write a brief on the best note-taking apps... comparing sync, export,
   privacy and pricing", t57/t58 "Explain.../Summarise the arguments..." with
   recent numbers, t60 "Overview of current interest rates...". It keys on the
   surface wording; gemma reads the substance.
2. **Latency does not change that.** Laya is ~17x faster warm (about 30 ms vs
   about 500 ms), but the router runs once per dispatched commitment, in front
   of a research job that takes minutes. 0.5 s warm, and 2-5 s cold, is noise at
   that point; it is not on the voice path. gemma is also the easy-task executor,
   so it is usually already resident, and it needs no second runtime (MLX) or
   extra dependency in the Delegator.
3. **Robustness.** 0 invalid / timeout / error outputs in 720 gemma calls
   across both full runs (JSON mode + `reasoning_effort="none"`). Failures would
   still resolve to `hard`.
4. **Stability.** All four configurations produced identical predictions in
   every repetition, and in both full runs (temperature 0 for gemma; Laya is
   deterministic). Stability is not a differentiator.

### Latency, two full runs
The committed `results.json` / table above is the second run, during which
other sessions were using Ollama (load average 2.3-3.4; `ollama ps` shows a
22 GB `nemotron-3.5-lightning` and gemma reloaded by someone else). That
inflated the tails: gemma warm p95 1.4 s, a 5.0 s cold call, Laya p95 175 ms in
one variant. The first full run, same code apart from where the environment
snapshot was taken (load average 1.0-1.7), was quieter:

| Router / variant | Warm p50 ms | Warm p95 ms | Cold first call ms (3 reps) | Laya load ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemma/v1 | 496 | 508 | 2090, 2068, 2086 | - |
| gemma/v2 | 557 | 591 | 2114, 2120, 2125 | - |
| laya/v1 | 29 | 30 | 68, 38, 43 | 324, 286, 287 |
| laya/v2 | 31 | 32 | 39, 49, 67 | 288, 285, 293 |

Accuracy numbers were identical in both runs. Treat the quiet-run numbers as
representative, and the second run as what contention with other Ollama work
does to gemma (Ollama queues requests per model; Laya on MLX is less affected).
"Cold" for gemma means evicted from Ollama (`keep_alive=0`) but with weights
still in the OS page cache; a true first-boot load from disk will be slower.

### Prompt variants (at most 2 per router, all reported)
- v1: descriptive criteria only. v2: the same plus generic anchor examples
  ("compare five databases on cost, latency and licensing", "explain what DNS
  is"), deliberately not copied from the set.
- gemma: v2 fixed its single miss (t60, current savings rates). One item is
  weak evidence; v2 is picked because it is no worse anywhere and its anchors
  spell out "fast-moving field" and "comparison", which is the rule we want.
  Its warm latency is ~60 ms higher (longer prompt).
- Laya: v2 did not help. Same 4 hard misses (t57 swapped in for t60), and
  it moved a clear easy item (t06, SSH keys how-to) to hard. Laya would need a
  different approach, e.g. fine-tuning on labelled commitments, or several
  narrower binary questions (needs recent data? compares many options?) OR-ed
  into `hard`. That was not tried here (variant budget).

### Caveats and overfitting risk
- One author wrote the labels, the criteria and the prompts, and gemma/v2 is
  100% on its own set. The set is more separable than real commitments will be.
  Expect lower accuracy in production; the gap between the routers on the
  borderline items (the part that is not trivially separable) is the more
  trustworthy signal than the headline numbers.
- 60 items, 14 borderline: one item moves hard recall by 3.2 points overall and
  12.5 points on the borderline subset. The v1/v2 difference for gemma is
  within that noise.
- Re-run with real dispatched commitments once there are ~50 (SPEC section 8.3)
  and keep the fail-safe (`hard` on any invalid output or timeout).
